## SECTION 1: THE FOUR MODERN ERAS OF F1

Formula 1 technical regulations dictate the exact dimensions, weight, and power units of the cars. Since 2005, there have been four distinct technical eras, each requiring entirely different aerodynamic and engineering philosophies.

### 1. The V8 Aerodynamic Era (2006–2013)

*   **Engine:** 2.4L Naturally Aspirated V8.
*   **Aero Philosophy:** Complex over-body aerodynamics. Teams attached countless winglets, bargeboards, and turning vanes to the cars to manage airflow.
*   **Signature Tech:** Exhaust-blown diffusers (routing hot exhaust gases over the floor to generate immense rear downforce) and F-Ducts (driver-stalled rear wings for straight-line speed).
*   **Dominant Team:** Red Bull Racing (perfected the blown diffuser).

### 2. The V6 Turbo-Hybrid Era (2014–2021)

*   **Engine:** 1.6L V6 Turbocharged with complex Energy Recovery Systems (MGU-K for kinetic energy, MGU-H for heat energy from the turbo).
*   **Aero Philosophy:** Simplified over-body aero initially, growing highly complex by 2021. Wide cars (2m) introduced in 2017 to drastically increase cornering speeds.
*   **Signature Tech:** The Power Unit itself. The thermal efficiency of these engines reached over 50% (compared to ~30% for a road car).
*   **Dominant Team:** Mercedes (mastered the split-turbo power unit design).

### 3. The Ground Effect Era (2022–2025)

*   **Engine:** 1.6L V6 Turbo-Hybrid (Engine development frozen until 2026).
*   **Aero Philosophy:** A complete revolution to improve overtaking. Over-body aerodynamics were heavily restricted. Downforce is now primarily generated by two massive venturi tunnels under the floor (Ground Effect). This reduces the "dirty air" wake behind the car, allowing cars to follow closely.
*   **Signature Tech:** 3D underfloor tunnels.
*   **Major Issue:** "Porpoising" — an aerodynamic phenomenon where the floor stalls at high speed, causing the car to bounce violently up and down.
*   **Dominant Team:** Red Bull Racing (mastered floor ride-height and stiffness).

### 4. The Active Aero Era (Coming in 2026)

*   **Engine:** 1.6L V6 Turbo-Hybrid, but with the MGU-H removed and a massive increase in electrical power (50% internal combustion, 50% electrical).
*   **Aero Philosophy:** Smaller, lighter cars with active aerodynamics (moving front and rear wings on straights to reduce drag).

## SECTION 2: THE POWER UNIT (2014–2025)

The modern F1 Power Unit (PU) is the most complex racing engine ever created. It consists of six distinct elements. Drivers are limited to a specific number of each element per season. Exceeding this limit results in grid penalties.

1.  **Internal Combustion Engine (ICE):** The 1.6-litre V6 block. (Limit: 4 per season).
2.  **Turbocharger (TC):** Compresses air into the engine. (Limit: 4 per season).
3.  **MGU-H (Motor Generator Unit - Heat):** Recovers energy from the exhaust gases spinning the turbo. Crucial for eliminating turbo lag. (Limit: 4 per season).
4.  **MGU-K (Motor Generator Unit - Kinetic):** Recovers kinetic energy under braking (similar to regenerative braking in road EVs). Deploys 120kW (~160 horsepower) of electrical boost. (Limit: 4 per season).
5.  **Energy Store (ES):** The battery that holds the recovered electrical energy. (Limit: 2 per season).
6.  **Control Electronics (CE):** The ECU that manages the complex energy flow. (Limit: 2 per season).

## SECTION 3: DRS (DRAG REDUCTION SYSTEM)

Introduced in 2011 to artificially aid overtaking.

*   **Mechanism:** An actuator opens a flap in the rear wing, dumping aerodynamic drag and increasing straight-line speed by 15-20 km/h.
*   **Rules:** Can only be used in designated DRS Zones.
*   **Race Condition:** The chasing car must be within 1.000 second of the car ahead at the DRS Detection Point.
*   **Qualifying Condition:** Drivers can use DRS in the designated zones on every lap, regardless of gaps.
*   **Safety:** Disabled for the first lap of a race, the first lap after a Safety Car, and in wet conditions.

## SECTION 4: CAR DIMENSIONS AND WEIGHT LIMITS

F1 cars have grown drastically in weight and length since 2005. This is due to the heavy hybrid power units, massively increased safety structures (the Halo), and larger wheels. The current cars are often described by drivers as feeling like "boats" in slow-speed corners compared to the nimble V8 era.

*   **2005 Weight Limit:** 605 kg
*   **2014 Weight Limit:** 691 kg (Introduction of Hybrids)
*   **2018 Weight Limit:** 733 kg (Introduction of the Halo safety device)
*   **2025 Weight Limit:** 798 kg (Current Generation)

**Dimensions (2025):**
*   **Width:** 2.0 metres
*   **Maximum Length:** Not strictly regulated, but wheelbase is capped at 3.6 metres (effectively resulting in cars ~5.5 metres long).
*   **Tyres:** 18-inch rims (introduced in 2022 to be more road-relevant, replacing the classic 13-inch rims).
